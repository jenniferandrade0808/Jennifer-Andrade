# Research Brief: Kauai Beef Supply and Processing Labor

Research Question: Why does Kauai remain heavily dependent on imported beef despite maintaining 42,345 pasture acres and nearly 14,000 cattle? While the initial hypothesis identified midstream processing labor as the primary bottleneck, field data revealed a sequential constraint. Under current market conditions, the upstream constraint binds first: the economic necessity of calf exports starves the local plant of harvestable livestock. However, the midstream processing capacity forms a hard, secondary ceiling that binds at just ~13% retention of exported calves. Therefore, Kauai's dependency is driven by upstream export economics, immediately backed by a rigid midstream processing ceiling.

## Planned Analysis

Planned Analysis: The final analysis evaluates Kauai's local beef supply chain using a sequential constraint framework. It first models the upstream opportunity cost of calf exports using discounted cash flow analysis against AMS feeder prices, then evaluates the midstream physical capacity ceiling utilizing primary operational data. The analysis concludes by modeling the fiscal impact of a dynamic state-funded price-bridge contract combined with immediate infrastructure capital grants.
