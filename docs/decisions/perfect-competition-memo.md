# Memorandum: Operational Planting Plan & Resource Allocation

**SUBJECT:** Seasonal Planting Recommendation & Resource Optimization (Baseline Mix)

---

### The Plan
Plant **10 beds of tomatoes, 20 beds of carrots, and 30 beds of mesclun**, leaving 4 beds idle. This allocation maximizes seasonal net profit at **$42,761.66** (`Model!C19`) while preventing severe labor penalties.

### Why This Mix Works
Gross revenue does not dictate planting decisions; marginal economics do. Tomatoes halt at 10 beds because the 11th bed's marginal cost ($9,390.72) exceeds the fixed $8,800 market price, yielding an immediate single-bed loss of $590.72 due to 10% compounding labor penalties.

Carrots and mesclun halt strictly because their 20- and 30-bed production caps bind. While standalone accounting models naively show negative profits by forcing each crop to shoulder the entire $20,000 fixed overhead alone, both crops comfortably pass the short-run shutdown rule (P ≥ AVC):
- **Carrots (20 beds):** Average Variable Cost is $1,918.45 against a $2,094.00 price.
- **Mesclun (30 beds):** Average Variable Cost is $2,430.74 against a $2,700.00 price.

Because price clears average variable cost across both planted blocks, every bed generates vital contribution margin toward the farm's sunk $20,000 fixed overhead.

### The Judgment Call (Resource & Land Allocation)
The 4 idle beds sit empty strictly due to crop-specific bed caps, not an overall shortage of land or temporary labor (headcount and total acreage remain economically slack at $0.00 shadow price). If management can negotiate or lease additional bed quota, prioritize carrots first:
- **Carrot Cap Expansion:** Shadow price is **$352.49 per bed**.
- **Mesclun Cap Expansion:** Shadow price is **$246.47 per bed**.

As demonstrated in our joint capacity run (`Model!F19`), expanding both bed caps by 4 beds absorbs all 4 idle beds (10/23/31) and adds **$1,138.83 in incremental net profit**, lifting seasonal earnings to **$43,900.49**.

### What Would Change My Answer
This operating plan is most sensitive to **tomato market prices**. A decline of just **6.3%** (dropping price below $8,248.59) causes bed 10's marginal cost to exceed market price, immediately cutting optimal tomato production to 9 beds. Conversely, any upward revision to carrot or mesclun bed caps immediately triggers planting on the 4 idle beds.
