Aloha Jennifer — this is a strong topic, and a Kauaʻi question is exactly the right size for this
paper. The framing that shipping disruptions *expose* the vulnerability rather than cause it is the
best idea in it; lead with that.

**Your first question — is labor-market clearing enough as the engine?** Keep it as the main
mechanism. Compensating wage differentials against the hospitality wage floor is the right tool, and
it's a real microeconomic argument. But don't give the calf-export decision equal weight as a second
mechanism; make it the rival you test. It's already your third falsification condition, and it's the
one most likely to bite: if weaned calves leave the island because mainland finishing pays ranchers
more, local plants are short of animals, not hands, and the labor story weakens. So the evidence the
paper most needs is how many animals are actually processed on-island versus shipped out, next to
what the local processing facilities could handle if staffed. If you can show plants turning animals
away or running short-handed while calves still ship, your mechanism holds.

**Your second question — is the scope narrow enough?** Because this question bears directly on your
own business, I'm making an exception on length: if going past four pages deepens your research and
your understanding of the problem, take the room. Your scope is narrow enough as it stands. The one
thing to guard against is breadth that thins the argument, so if you need to trim anywhere, trim here
first: the resilience externality can live in the policy section, where it justifies the retainer,
rather than as a second mechanism; and the capacity figure is strongest when its headline comparison
is the processing throughput ceiling against island demand, with the herd-offtake tier alongside it.
Keep the two objections to your policy; that's the strongest part of the design.

**One housekeeping note.** Give the 85–90 percent import figure a primary source when you write it
up, the same way you've pinned the HDOA and NASS tables.

**In order:**

1. Make calf exports the rival you test, and find the on-island versus shipped-out numbers.
2. Make throughput against demand the headline comparison in your capacity figure.
3. Source the import benchmark.
4. Use the extra length where it deepens the argument; don't spread it thinner.

You're clear to lock the spec and start drafting.
